## The 10-year benefit, briefly

<TermChip he="עולה חדש" en="new immigrant" /> <TermChip he="תושב חוזר ותיק" en="veteran returning resident" />

When you make aliyah (or return after at least ten years abroad as a *veteran returning resident*), Israel doesn't tax your **foreign-source income** for 10 years: things like US rental income, dividends from a US brokerage account, or a US pension. Income you earn by working in Israel is taxed normally.

## What changed in 2026

Until now, olim didn't have to **report** that foreign income or their foreign assets either. Amendment 272 to the Income Tax Ordinance ended the reporting exemption for anyone who becomes an Israeli resident **on or after January 1, 2026**:

- The 10-year **tax** exemption on foreign income stays.
- You must now **report** your worldwide income and foreign assets on an Israeli annual return, even though the foreign income isn't taxed.
- People who became residents before 2026 keep the old rules for the rest of their benefit period.

<Callout type="warning">Reporting means filing an Israeli annual return each year, often with details of US accounts. Ask an Israeli accountant early, ideally in your first year, rather than when the first return is due.</Callout>

## The new 2026 tax break on Israeli work

A temporary law passed by the Knesset in March 2026 exempts part of the income from **work performed in Israel** for new olim and veteran returning residents who became Israeli residents between **late 2025 and December 31, 2026**. The exempt amount is capped:

| Tax year | Cap on exempt income |
| --- | --- |
| 2026 | ₪600,000 (prorated for the part of the year you lived here) |
| 2027 | ₪1,000,000 |
| 2028 | ₪1,000,000 |
| 2029 | ₪350,000 |
| 2030 | ₪150,000 |

The exact start date and conditions come from the final law and Tax Authority guidance. Confirm your eligibility with an Israeli accountant.

## What it means for your US return

None of this changes your US taxes. The US still taxes your worldwide income. One side effect: if Israel taxes your salary less, you have less Israeli tax to use as a [Foreign Tax Credit](/learn/us-taxes/feie-or-foreign-tax-credit), so the exclusion may suit you better in those years.
