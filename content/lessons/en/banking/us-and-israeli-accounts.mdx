## Why two accounts

- **Israeli account (חשבון עו״ש):** your salary, rent, bills, Bituach Leumi payments and child allowance all go through it.
- **US account:** for income paid in dollars (US clients, a US pension, rent from US property), paying US bills and taxes, moving money to a US brokerage, and keeping a US financial footprint if you might move back.

## What to expect from an Israeli bank

When you open an account, the bank asks whether you're a US citizen and usually asks you to sign a **W-9** form. Under the US–Israel FATCA agreement, Israeli banks report US citizens' accounts to the Israel Tax Authority, which shares them with the IRS. This is routine. It's one more reason to file your [FBAR](/learn/fbar-fatca/fbar-reporting) correctly, since the IRS can already see the account.

## What to expect from a US bank

Some US banks limit or close accounts when the customer lives abroad. Before you move, ask your bank directly whether it supports customers with an Israeli address, and update your address honestly.

<Callout type="warning">Using a relative's US address so a bank thinks you live in the US can break the account terms and cause trouble later. Choose a bank that accepts your real address.</Callout>

## Reporting

| Account | FBAR | Israeli reporting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Israeli account | Yes, it's a foreign account | Usually none for a plain account |
| US account | No, it's not foreign to the US | May need reporting if you file an Israeli return, especially from 2026 for new olim |
