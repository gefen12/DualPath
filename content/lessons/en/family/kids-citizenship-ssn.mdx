## Step 1: Is your child a US citizen?

<TermChip he="דיווח על לידה בחו״ל" en="CRBA" />

If you're married to a non-American, your child is generally a US citizen at birth if you were **physically present in the US for at least five years before the birth, at least two of them after age 14**. Different rules apply if both parents are American, or the parents aren't married. The State Department page in the sources has the details.

Proof of time in the US can be school records, transcripts, tax returns or employment records. Start collecting them before the appointment.

## Step 2: The CRBA

The **Consular Report of Birth Abroad** is the US document showing your child became a citizen at birth. You apply through the US Embassy in Jerusalem or its Tel Aviv branch office, usually together with the child's first US passport. Doing it while the child is young is much easier than later.

## Step 3: The Social Security number

The CRBA (or the US passport) is proof of citizenship for an **SSN** application. The SSN is what lets you claim your child on your US return.

## Step 4: The child tax credit

For 2025, the **child tax credit** is up to **$2,200 per qualifying child**, and up to **$1,700** of it is **refundable**, meaning paid to you even when you owe no US tax.

<Callout type="warning">If you claim the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (Form 2555), you can't get the refundable part. Many families in Israel receive it only by using the Foreign Tax Credit instead. See [FEIE or Foreign Tax Credit?](/learn/us-taxes/feie-or-foreign-tax-credit)</Callout>

## The Israeli side: savings for every child

Bituach Leumi deposits **₪58 a month** (from January 2026) into a savings plan for each child, and parents can add another ₪58 from the child allowance. You choose a bank account or a kupat gemel track. For an American child, the kupat gemel track raises the same PFIC question as other Israeli funds, which is why the bank option is often suggested.
