## Israel: no inheritance tax

Israel abolished its estate tax in 1981 and has no inheritance tax today. Selling inherited assets can still have Israeli tax consequences, for example on a property, so check before you sell.

## The US: estate tax for citizens, but a high threshold

The US taxes the worldwide estate of a US citizen, but only above the **basic exclusion amount**: **$15 million per person for deaths in 2026**. Most families never reach it.

## Receiving money from abroad

If you receive **more than $100,000 in a year** in gifts or inheritances from **non-Americans** (for example an Israeli parent or grandparent), you must report it on **Form 3520**. There's no tax on receiving it, but the penalty for not reporting is 5% of the amount per month, up to 25%.

## Inheriting investments

<Callout type="warning">If you inherit Israeli mutual funds, ETFs or a kupat gemel, you inherit their PFIC status too. Talk to a CPA before deciding whether to keep or sell them.</Callout>

## Planning ahead

- Wills that work in both countries are worth the effort, especially for a mixed US–Israeli family.
- Keep a list of accounts, pensions and policies, with who to contact, somewhere your family can find it.
