## Your filing options

- **Married filing separately.** The usual choice. You report only your own income. Your spouse has no US filing at all.
- **Married filing jointly, by election.** You can both elect to treat your non-American spouse as a US resident. Then **both** of your worldwide incomes are taxed by the US, and you generally can't claim treaty benefits as Israeli residents for that year. Your spouse needs an SSN or ITIN.
- **Head of household.** In some cases, for example with a qualifying child living with you, a US citizen married to a non-resident can file as head of household, which has better rates than married filing separately. Ask your preparer whether you qualify.

<Callout type="info">Filing jointly sometimes lowers the bill, for example when your spouse has little income. But it brings their income, accounts and investments into the US system. Compare both ways before choosing.</Callout>

## Accounts you share

- **Joint accounts** with your spouse are your accounts for the FBAR. Report each one at its full highest balance.
- Accounts only in your spouse's name aren't on your FBAR, unless you can sign on them.

## Investments and savings in your spouse's name

Your spouse isn't subject to US rules if you file separately, so Israeli funds in their name don't create PFIC problems for you. Owning things jointly, or electing joint filing, changes that.

## Gifts between you

Gifts from a US citizen to a non-citizen spouse have a lower annual tax-free limit than gifts between US citizen spouses. If you're moving large sums, such as a home purchase, into your spouse's name, ask your CPA first.
