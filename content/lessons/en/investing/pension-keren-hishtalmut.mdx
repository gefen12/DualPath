## What they are

<TermChip he="קרן פנסיה" en="pension fund" /> <TermChip he="קרן השתלמות" en="study fund" />

- **Pension fund (קרן פנסיה):** mandatory retirement savings for employees in Israel, paid in by you and your employer, with large Israeli tax benefits.
- **Keren hishtalmut (קרן השתלמות):** a savings fund paid into by you and your employer. In Israel, it can be withdrawn tax-free after six years (or earlier for some purposes), within limits.

## Why US treatment is debated

The US has no rule written specifically for these funds. Professionals take different positions, for example:

- treating them like a **foreign pension**, with tax deferred until you take the money out;
- treating the keren hishtalmut as a **foreign trust**, with extra reporting;
- or taxing **employer contributions or growth** each year.

The US–Israel tax treaty has rules on pensions, but how they apply to each fund is part of the debate.

## What most people do in practice

- **Keep the pension.** It's mandatory for employees, and leaving it isn't usually an option.
- **Decide about the keren hishtalmut with a CPA**, weighing the employer's contribution against the US reporting and possible US tax.
- **Report them consistently** every year: on the FBAR, and wherever your preparer's approach requires.
- **Watch the investment track.** Funds inside these accounts are generally Israeli funds, which feeds the PFIC debate. Some people choose simpler tracks to reduce it.

<Callout type="warning">Don't stop contributing or withdraw early just because of US worries. That can cost you the employer's contribution and Israeli tax benefits. Get advice first.</Callout>
