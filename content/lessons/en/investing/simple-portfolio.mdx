## Building blocks that are usually fine for Americans

## Three layers

1. **Cash you might need soon:** an emergency fund in a bank deposit (פיקדון) or Makam (מק״מ). Not a money-market fund (קרן כספית), which is an Israeli fund and so a PFIC.
2. **Shekel savings for the medium term:** Israeli government bonds, Shachar (fixed rate) or Galil (inflation-linked), bought directly, not through a bond fund.
3. **Long-term growth:** broad, low-cost US-listed index funds, held through a broker that allows them. See [Choosing a broker](/learn/investing/choosing-a-broker).

## Currency

You earn and spend in shekels, but US-listed funds are in dollars. Many people keep part of their savings in each currency. There's no single right split. It depends on where you expect to spend the money.

<Callout type="info">This is general education, not a recommendation for you. How much to put in each layer depends on your age, income, family and plans. A licensed investment adviser can help, ideally one who knows US rules.</Callout>
