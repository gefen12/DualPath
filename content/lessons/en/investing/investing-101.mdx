## Three questions for every investment

1. **What is it?** A single stock or bond you own directly, or a fund that pools many investments?
2. **Where is it held?** An Israeli bank or broker, or a US brokerage account?
3. **Who taxes it?** As an Israeli resident you're taxed in Israel; as a US citizen you're taxed in the US too. The [Foreign Tax Credit](/learn/us-taxes/feie-or-foreign-tax-credit) usually stops the same gain being taxed twice in full.

## The one rule that matters most

<TermChip he="קרן נאמנות" en="mutual fund" /> <TermChip he="קרן סל" en="ETF" />

To the IRS, almost any **fund that isn't American** is a **PFIC**, and PFICs are taxed harshly. That includes Israeli mutual funds and ETFs, even ones that simply track the S&P 500. [Why Israeli funds are a trap](/learn/investing/israeli-funds-trap) explains why.

## How Israel taxes investments

Israel generally taxes capital gains on securities at **25%** of the real (inflation-adjusted) gain, and more for large shareholders. An Israeli bank or broker usually withholds that tax for you. If you invest through a **broker outside Israel**, nobody withholds, and you'll usually have to file an Israeli annual return yourself.

## How the US taxes them

US-listed funds, stocks and bonds are taxed the ordinary US way: dividends and gains go on your return, with lower rates for long-term gains. The Israeli tax you paid on the same income can usually be claimed as a credit.

<Callout type="info">Already own Israeli funds? Don't sell in a hurry. Selling can trigger the PFIC tax, so plan it with a CPA.</Callout>
