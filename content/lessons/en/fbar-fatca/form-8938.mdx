## FBAR and Form 8938 are different

| | FBAR | Form 8938 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Filed with | FinCEN, online | Your tax return (IRS) |
| Threshold abroad (single) | More than $10,000 at any time | More than $200,000 at year end or $300,000 at any time |
| Threshold abroad (married filing jointly) | More than $10,000 at any time | More than $400,000 at year end or $600,000 at any time |
| Due | April 15, extended to October 15 | With the return |

Meeting one doesn't excuse the other. Many people in Israel only ever file the FBAR, because their assets stay below the Form 8938 thresholds.

## Who counts as "living abroad"

The higher thresholds apply if you were a **bona fide resident** of another country for the whole tax year, or **physically present** outside the US for at least **330 full days** in a 12-month period ending that year. Most Americans living in Israel qualify.

## What goes on it

Foreign financial accounts, like those on the FBAR, plus other foreign financial assets such as shares held directly in a foreign company. Your Israeli home isn't reported, because real estate held directly isn't a financial asset.

<Callout type="info">Israeli banks report accounts of US citizens to the Israel Tax Authority, which passes the information to the IRS under the US–Israel FATCA agreement. The IRS can usually already see these accounts.</Callout>
