## Do you need to file yet?

You must file a US return for a year if your **gross income** reached the filing threshold. For 2025, for a single person under 65, that's **$15,750**. Gross income means your pay before any exclusion, converted to dollars, plus things like interest.

Two other triggers:

- **Self-employment:** net earnings of **$400 or more** mean you must file, whatever your total income. Freelancing or a side business counts.
- **Foreign accounts:** the FBAR has its own test (more than $10,000 in total at any point), and it applies even if you don't need a tax return.

<Callout type="info">Below the threshold you don't have to file, but it's still worth keeping a record of your income and account balances for each year. It makes later years, and any catch-up, much easier.</Callout>

## What a simple first return looks like

For a soldier, a student with a part-time job, or someone in their first salaried job, the return usually has:

- **Form 1040**, the return itself.
- Your Israeli pay, from your **Form 106** (the annual salary statement from your employer), converted to dollars.
- **Either Form 2555** (Foreign Earned Income Exclusion) **or Form 1116** (Foreign Tax Credit) to remove the US tax on that pay. Which one is better is the subject of [the next lesson](/learn/us-taxes/feie-or-foreign-tax-credit).
- Bank interest, if any, also converted to dollars.

## When it's due

- The return is due **June 15**, an automatic 2-month extension for people living abroad.
- Any tax owed was due **April 15**, so interest runs from then.
- Filing **Form 4868** by June 15 gives you until **October 15**.

## Things that trip people up

- **Using the wrong address on purpose.** Use your real Israeli address. Your filing dates and options depend on where you live.
- **Forgetting the FBAR** because the tax return showed zero tax.
- **Choosing the exclusion without thinking ahead.** If you later have children, the exclusion blocks the refundable child tax credit. Read the next lesson before you pick.
