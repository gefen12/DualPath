## The two tools

<TermChip he="החרגת הכנסה מעבודה בחו״ל" en="FEIE" /> <TermChip he="זיכוי מס זר" en="Foreign Tax Credit" />

- **Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE, Form 2555).** You exclude your foreign salary from US income, up to **$132,900 for 2026** ($130,000 for 2025). To use it you must live abroad as a *bona fide resident* for a full tax year, or be physically outside the US for **330 full days** in a 12-month period. It only covers earned income, such as salary or self-employment profit, and not interest, dividends or capital gains.
- **Foreign Tax Credit (FTC, Form 1116).** You report all your income, work out the US tax, then subtract the income tax you paid to Israel on that income. Credit you can't use this year can be carried **back 1 year and forward 10 years**.

## Why the credit often wins in Israel

Israeli income tax rates on a salary are generally higher than US rates on the same salary. So the Israeli tax you paid is usually more than enough to cancel the US tax, and the leftover credit carries forward. That carryover can later shelter other foreign income.

<Callout type="warning">If you claim the FEIE, you **can't claim the additional (refundable) child tax credit**. For 2025 that refundable part is worth up to $1,700 per child. Many parents in Israel get it in cash only when they use the Foreign Tax Credit.</Callout>

## When the exclusion can make sense

- Your Israeli tax is low, for example because of oleh tax points or a partial year, so the credit wouldn't fully cover the US tax.
- You have no children and want a simpler return.
- Your income is well below the exclusion limit, and you don't need FTC carryovers.

## Switching later

You choose the exclusion each year by filing Form 2555. But if you **revoke** it, you generally can't choose it again for **5 years** without IRS approval. Claiming the refundable child tax credit in a later year counts as revoking it. So think about where your family will be in a few years before you start with the exclusion.

<Callout type="info">Both tools only deal with income tax. Neither one removes US self-employment (Social Security) tax. See [Self-employed: Bituach Leumi + US tax](/learn/israeli-taxes/self-employed-bituach-leumi).</Callout>
