## Why Americans in Israel file at all

The US taxes its citizens on their worldwide income, wherever they live. Moving to Israel, or being born here to an American parent, doesn't change that. Israel taxes you too, as a resident. The two systems are separate, so you deal with each one on its own.

The good news: for most people with an ordinary Israeli salary, the US return ends with little or no US tax to pay, because of the [Foreign Earned Income Exclusion or the Foreign Tax Credit](/learn/us-taxes/feie-or-foreign-tax-credit). But you still have to file to claim them.

## The US side

- **Form 1040, the tax return.** You must file if your gross income is at or above the filing threshold. For a single person under 65, that was $15,750 for 2025. Count your salary before any exclusion. If you're self-employed, you must file once your net self-employment earnings reach $400.
- **FBAR (FinCEN Form 114).** If your accounts outside the US added up to more than $10,000 at any moment of the year, you report them all. It's filed online with FinCEN, separately from the tax return. See [FBAR: reporting your Israeli accounts](/learn/fbar-fatca/fbar-reporting).
- **Form 8938 (FATCA)** goes with the tax return once your foreign assets pass a higher threshold. See [Form 8938](/learn/fbar-fatca/form-8938).

## The dates

| Date | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| April 15 | Any US tax you owe is due. Interest runs from here, even if you file later. The FBAR is also due, but it's extended to October 15 automatically. |
| June 15 | Automatic 2-month extension to file Form 1040 for people living abroad. |
| October 15 | Last date for Form 1040 if you filed Form 4868 by June 15, and the FBAR's extended due date. |
| December 31 | Note your highest balance in each account this year. You'll need it for the FBAR. |

<Callout type="info">Since October 2023 the IRS has postponed many US deadlines for people whose main home is in Israel, most recently for deadlines up to September 30, 2027 (Notice 2026-63). A postponement isn't a cancellation, and FBAR relief is announced separately by FinCEN. Check with your CPA before relying on it.</Callout>

## The Israeli side

Most salaried employees in Israel don't file an annual return: the employer withholds tax and that's the end of it. You may have to file one if, for example, you're self-employed, you have income from abroad, or you invest through a broker outside Israel. The usual date is the end of April, but the Israel Tax Authority often extends it: for 2025 returns it moved to May 31, or June 30 for online filers.
