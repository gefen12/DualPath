## Who it's for

Many Americans in Israel never filed: they moved as children, were born here to an American parent, or simply assumed that living abroad meant no US taxes. The IRS has a procedure built for exactly this, as long as the failure was **non-willful**: a mistake, a misunderstanding, or not knowing the rules.

If you already knew you had to file and chose not to, this procedure isn't for you. Talk to a professional about the other options before you file anything.

## What you file

Under the **Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures** you send:

- **Three years of US tax returns** (Form 1040), the most recent three whose due dates have passed, with all the forms they need, such as Form 2555 or Form 1116.
- **Six years of FBARs**, filed online with FinCEN.
- **Form 14653**, signed. In it you certify that the failure was non-willful and explain, in your own words, why it happened.
- **Payment** of any tax and interest those three returns show.

## Do you qualify as "living abroad"?

For a US citizen, you need at least one of the last three years in which you had no US home ("abode") and were physically outside the US for at least 330 full days. Most people who live in Israel meet this easily. If you file jointly, both spouses must meet it.

If you don't, you fall into the **Streamlined Domestic** procedure, which has a 5% penalty.

<Callout type="info">For people who qualify as living abroad, the Streamlined Foreign Offshore procedure has no offshore penalty, and the returns aren't charged failure-to-file or failure-to-pay penalties. You still pay any tax due, with interest.</Callout>

## Why not just start filing this year?

Filing only from now on leaves earlier years open. The Streamlined procedure closes them in a recognized way. Most people with Israeli salaries end up owing little or nothing for those years, because the exclusion or the foreign tax credit wipes out the US tax. The real cost is usually the preparer's fee.

<Callout type="warning">The non-willful statement is signed under penalty of perjury. Write it honestly and specifically, and have a CPA read it before you sign.</Callout>
